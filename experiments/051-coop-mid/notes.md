## Incumbent shape budget (`results/inc-890798-shapediag.json`)

Geomean of the 15 per-shape walls is 820.08us. Ratio to the hardware floor,
worst first: 4x1024 147x, 2x2048 141x, 16x512 84x, 2x4096 42x, 8x2048 42x,
1x4096 40x — against 1x32768 at only 2.2x. The loss is dependent-pivot latency
plus low-intensity Schur work, not FLOPs and not launch count.

Two findings.

**The single-extension merge is free.** Both unenrolled control shapes came back
at 1.0006 and 1.0011 with tight CIs, independently reproducing experiment 050
V6's result. Collapsing the four `load_inline` extensions into one — which is
what makes any CUDA change survive popcorn's 360s cold-build budget — costs
nothing, including for `chol256`, whose `-O2` became `-O3`.

**The rank-32 trailing update is the wall.** Subtracting the known serial
diagonal chain (nt x 6.2us) from each wall and dividing the shape's total
Cholesky FLOPs by what is left:

| shape | nt | diagonal chain | residual time | implied trailing rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 16x512 | 16 | 99us | ~280us | 2.6 TFLOP/s |
| 4x1024 | 32 | 198us | ~550us | 2.6 TFLOP/s |
| 2x2048 | 64 | 397us | ~1350us | 4.2 TFLOP/s |
| 8x2048 | 64 | 397us | ~2643us | 8.7 TFLOP/s |
| 1x4096 | 128 | 794us | ~3554us | 6.4 TFLOP/s |
| 2x4096 | 128 | 794us | ~5835us | 7.9 TFLOP/s |

That explains the shape of the loss exactly: the ratio degrades monotonically
with trailing volume, from 1.037x at 16x512 to 0.352x at 1x4096. It is a
structural ceiling, not a tuning miss — a rank-32 update of 32x32 tiles reads
three tiles and writes one for 65 kFLOP, about **4 flops per byte**, which caps
it near 28 TFLOP/s even at full HBM bandwidth. Loading the MMA fragments
straight from global memory (inherited from 048 V2) gives up most of even that.

The panel repair itself worked: 16x512, where the serial chain is the largest
share of the budget, is the one shape that improved.

**Conclusion carried into v2:** keep the dependent-chain design, block the
factorization at NB=128 so the bandwidth-bound work becomes a rank-128 update
at ~16 flops/byte, and stage its operands through shared memory.

| shape | control us | v1b us | ratio | v1 ratio |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 4x1024 | 692.2 | 740.1 | 0.9353 | 0.9575 |
| 2x2048 | 1367.9 | 2349.5 | 0.5821 | 0.7812 |
| 8x2048 | 1570.6 | 3265.8 | 0.4809 | 0.5292 |
| 1x4096 | 1535.7 | 12076.8 | 0.1272 | 0.3524 |
| 2x4096 | 3212.5 | 12512.9 | 0.2567 | 0.4833 |

Strictly worse everywhere, by up to a further 2.8x at 1x4096. **This closes the
grid-barrier hypothesis**: barrier cost falls with CTA count, so if barriers were
the constraint this variant would have won. Co-resident CTA count — i.e. how much
of the GPU the trailing update gets — is the binding constraint instead. It also
explains why experiment 048 V2's 32-CTA configuration was never going to scale
past 4x1024.

Two negative results worth keeping.

**Raising arithmetic intensity did not raise the rate.** At 1x4096 the implied
trailing rate is unchanged from v1 (~6 TFLOP/s) even though the rank-128 update
moves ~4x fewer bytes per flop. Whatever limits this kernel is therefore not
HBM bandwidth, which was v2's entire premise.

**Occupancy is not the limiter either.** v2b lifts the CTA cap from 256 to the
full occupancy-derived grid, which at batch 1 is a 2.3x increase in resident
CTAs, and 1x4096 got *worse* (0.2899 vs 0.3161). Combined with v1b, both
resource-side explanations — barriers and occupancy — are now closed.

v2 is additionally worse than v1 at small n, where its extra per-panel barrier
and its narrow in-panel updates buy nothing because the trailing volume is small.

Remaining candidate explanations, in the order the phase probe will separate
them: the in-kernel MMA path itself is latency-bound (`wmma::load_matrix_sync`
against a large leading dimension issues one transaction per row), or the
serial diagonal chain is far more expensive than experiment 048 V2's phase
table implied.
